↗NEXT MOVE

What should I make next?

  1. Make a sequel when the previous game is old enough and you have a newer engine.
  2. Take a publishing deal only when its compatibility is not a trap.
  3. Otherwise make a new game your fanbase can support.

↺QUALITY LOOP

Do not repeat blindly

Repeating the exact same topic + genre can reduce review scores. Rotate between strong pairs, and use new topics and combinations to gain experience and unlock research faster.

Change the pair, not necessarily the genre.
◌TEAM & SCALE

Respect the game size

Medium, Large and AAA games need increasingly higher T/D output. Train specialists for AAA, keep workloads under control, and use the strongest graphics your engine supports.

Small 1.0×Medium 1.2×Large 1.4×AAA 1.8×
≈REVIEW ENGINE

Protect your future score

Reviews are compared with your own Target High Score. Do not inflate it too quickly with unnecessary features: a steady run just above the 9 breakpoint is often safer than chasing a perfect 10 every time.

Your last best game changes the difficulty of the next one.
⊕MIXED GAMES

Mix with a plan

For multi-genre games, the first genre carries more weight than the second. For multi-platform games, choose the strongest platform first; extra reach can be worth a small review trade-off.

Best fit→Second fit→More market share
◷LATE GAME

Large, AAA and MMO checklist

✓ Large games: keep making Medium games until your fanbase and team can support the jump.

✓ AAA: use at least 3D Graphics V5 and three specialists in important design aspects.

✓ MMO: use an MMO Support engine and a +++ topic/genre pair.

✓ Expansions: wait at least 40 weeks between releases to avoid the major quality hit.

✓ G3: time development so phase 2 overlaps the June convention boost.

✓ Custom hardware: keep your console tech level aligned with the current generation.
